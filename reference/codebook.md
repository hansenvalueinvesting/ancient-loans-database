# ALD Codebook

Field definitions for the Ancient Loans Database. The data is `ald.csv` (repository root), one row per loan, columns in the order below. The previous build (13 fields incl. period, currency, source_url, notes) is archived in `ald1.csv`; its codebook is in git history.

## General rules

- **Standing rules.** Periods and other classifications follow their academic definitions (Hansen, Oct 2026). Only transactions that are strictly loans are recorded: financial arrangements where an entity provides money to another with the expectation of repayment over time, often including interest as a cost of borrowing (Hansen, Oct 2026).
- **What is a loan.** A loan is when someone borrows money (or goods) from a lender with the intention of returning it, with or without interest. Whether a transaction is a loan depends on the transaction itself, not on whether the source uses the word for loan: payments for goods or services (sales on credit, wages, work advances) are not loans even when called so.
- **What counts.** The database is a ledger of loans actually made: every loan transaction a source documents, none missed and none repeated, each individual loan recorded separately. Loan contracts (in money or in kind), loans mentioned in petitions or letters, loans copied or abstracted in contract registers, and earlier loans a document refers to (including loans it annuls or replaces). Court records only where they clearly show a loan not otherwise recorded. Not included: state loans of grain to cultivators, and arrangements not called a loan (pawns, loans of animals or tools, advances for work, antichretic leases). The source need not use the word "loan": what decides is whether the text shows a loan that was actually made. A loan already recorded from another document is not entered again.
- **One row per loan.** A loan is one credit transaction. If a source records several loans, each gets its own row. A contract that lends both money and goods (e.g. drachmas and wheat) gets one row per part.
- **Empty means unknown.** Leave a field empty when the source does not say. Never guess.
- **Record what the source says.** Do not convert or interpret values beyond what each field asks for.

## loans

Structure set by Hansen (Nov 2026), standardized for analysis (charts, maps). Ten fields, in this order (column names as written in the CSV header) (Hansen: volume and currency in separate columns so downloaded data is usable; the source is the first part of `notes`).

| Field | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `ALD-` + the citation of the edition that prints the text (scholarly practice: a papyrus only described in its first publication is cited by its later full edition, e.g. `ALD-SB.10.10246-1` for SB 10 10246 = P.Oxy. 2 304 descr.; Hansen, Nov 2026) + `-` + the loan's number within that source (1, 2, 3, ... in the order the loans appear in the source). In the citation, spaces become dots, superscript digits plain digits, and a journal's year in brackets is left out (`ALD-ZPE.222.179-1` for ZPE 222 (2022) 179). Fixed at entry: never changed (even if the source is later re-edited or renumbered) and never reused (Hansen, Nov 2026) | `ALD-P.Oxy.3.506-1`, `ALD-P.Oxy.3.506-2`, `ALD-IG.I3.369-14`, `ALD-Dem.35.10-1` |
| `borrower` | Name(s) of the borrower(s), in Latinized English form, `X son of Y` / `X daughter of Y`, `alias Z`; separate several with `; ` | `Tryphon`, `Dioscorus son of Sarapion` |
| `lender` | Name(s) of the lender(s), in the same form as `borrower` | `Thaisous` |
| `volume` | Principal lent, number only (Hansen, Nov 2026: "volume", not "amount"): whole number or fraction in lowest terms, never decimals, never converted. A sum in several units: one number per unit, separated by `; `, in the source's order, matching `currency` position by position (Hansen confirmed). Lost part: `[...]` where the loss is | `100`, `12 1/6`, `2/3`, `2; 4800`, `53; 2`, `1 [...]` |
| `currency` | Currency or unit of `volume`, singular, lowercase, as the source gives it; commodity of a loan in kind, or the metal the source names, in brackets; several units separated by `; `, matching `volume` | `drachma`, `artaba (wheat)`, `talent; drachma`, `drachma; obol`, `drachma (silver)` |
| `interest (%)` | Interest rate in percent per year, a number (simple interest: a monthly rate times 12; e.g. 1 drachma per mina per month = 12). A loan the text calls interest-free = `0`. If no rate is stated but the text gives both the sum lent and the sum to be repaid with the dates, the rate is calculated as (sum repaid - sum lent) / sum lent / duration in years, also for loans repaid in instalments, and the calculation shown in the notes (`Interest: calculated, not stated: ...`) (Hansen, Nov 2026); a range if the duration is a range; otherwise empty. The rate as the text writes it, and any other terms (interest-bearing without a rate, when interest is paid), go in the notes (`Interest: ...`) (Hansen, Nov 2026) | `12`, `6`, `8`, `1.68-1.75` |
| `date` | Year the loan was made, `AD n` or `n BC` (no year 0); uncertain: the range of possible years `AD 101-200`, `24-23 BC`, `30 BC-AD 14`. An earlier loan named in a later document gets its own year; if the source does not date the loan, the document's year, said in the notes (Hansen, Nov 2026) | `AD 57`, `100 BC`, `AD 101-200` |
| `duration (years)` | Term of the loan in years, a number with two decimals, counted from the day the loan was made to the day repayment is due (days / 365.25), dates converted to the Julian calendar. If either date is known only within limits (a month, a lost month), the range from the shortest to the longest possible term. Empty if the text does not give both dates or a term. The term as the text writes it goes in the notes (`Term: ...`) (Hansen, Nov 2026: "calculate the duration in years, and then put that down as a number") | `0.28`, `1.00`, `1.92-2.00` |
| `place` | Where the loan was made, by its ancient name, Latinized (`Heracleopolis`); a village may name its district in brackets. If the source does not say where the loan was made, the place of the document (papyri: HGV), said in the notes (Hansen, Nov 2026) | `Oxyrhynchus`, `Sinary (Oxyrhynchite nome)` |
| `notes` | Sections in this order, separated by a blank line (Hansen, Nov 2026): (1) `Source: <citation> (<link>)`: the citation used in the ID (the edition that prints the text), followed by the first publication in brackets when it differs, marked `descr.` if it only described the papyrus (`SB 10 10246 (= P.Oxy. 2 304 descr.)`); the link goes to that text, in standard form (papyri: Checklist, arabic volume numbers; inscriptions: corpus citation; literary texts: author, work, passage), required, followed by the link to its online edition in brackets if there is one; shown on the loan's page as a link; (2) `Original Text:` and the original text, line by line with line numbers, copied from the edition in its Leiden notation; (3) `English translation:` and a faithful translation, line by line with the same numbers; (4) short remarks, one per line, no heading, nothing that repeats the fields (Hansen, Nov 2026: the notes hold anything the standardized columns do not show): the lines that record the loan, the term as written (`Term: until ...`), and how date and place were found (always when the date is the document's year or the place is the document's place: `Date: not stated; the year of the document.`, `Place: not stated; the place of the document.`); (5) the credit line for every outside source used (text, metadata), with its licence (Hansen: "always give credit for anything outsourced"). Shown only on the loan's own page | `Source: P.Oxy. 3 506 (https://papyri.info/ddbdp/p.oxy;3;506)`<br><br>`Original Text:`<br>`...`<br><br>`English translation:`<br>`...` |
