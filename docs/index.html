What is a loan?

The working definition this database uses for every source.

A loan is a transaction in which one party (the lender) transfers money or other fungible goods (grain, wine, oil, metal) to another party (the borrower), who takes ownership and may spend or consume them, and who is obliged to return an equivalent quantity of the same kind at a later time, with or without an added payment for the use (interest). This is the Roman mutuum and the Greek daneion, the core form of credit in the scholarship on the ancient economy.

The test: all four must hold

  1. Transfer. Money or fungible goods actually pass from lender to borrower; a promise, request or plan is not enough.
  2. Use by the borrower. The borrower may spend or consume them. Lending an object to be handed back itself (a loan for use) is not credit.
  3. Obligation to repay an equivalent: the same quantity and kind, not a counter-performance in work or other goods.
  4. Deferral. Repayment falls at a later time, fixed, on demand or open.

Interest is not required, and may be replaced by another benefit to the lender (use of property, a person's service). The source need not use the word "loan". Lenders and borrowers may be persons, groups, cities, states, temples or rulers.

Evidence standard

  • Specific: an identifiable transaction, or an identifiable set of them by one lender or to one borrower.
  • Real: presented by the source as having happened; fiction and myth are not used.
  • Shown in the preserved text: a loan supplied only by an editor's restoration is not shown.
  • One record per transaction: money and goods lent in one contract are recorded separately; the same loan in several sources is recorded once.
